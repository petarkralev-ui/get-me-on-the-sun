We book and manage your NIE appointment early

The NIE, your foreigner identification number in Spain, is needed for many important steps, including the mortgage, notary, bank account, taxes, and property paperwork. It can be requested in Spain through the relevant Foreigners' Office or police station, or from abroad through the Spanish consulate for your country or area of residence. In practice, appointment availability, document requirements, and processing times can vary, so the key is to plan it early and choose the route that fits your travel dates and purchase timeline. We build the property search around real bank limits

Spanish mortgage lending is based on the bank's risk policy, your affordability, and the official valuation of the property, not only the advertised sale price. Resident buyers may sometimes be considered for higher financing, often around 80% of the valuation, while non-resident buyers should usually plan more conservatively, often around 60-70%, depending on the bank and the file. NIE or Residence

We identify the right document before booking

People often call every Spanish foreign-national document an NIE, but an identification number, an EU registration certificate, and a TIE residence card serve different purposes. We check your nationality, whether you are buying or moving, and your intended time in Spain before preparing the application.

NIE

Buying without moving to Spain

Most overseas buyers need an NIE number for the property, mortgage, bank, tax, and notary process. This is commonly called the "white NIE", but officially it is the assignment of an NIE. It is an identification number, not permission to live in Spain.

The usual file includes form EX-15, valid identification, evidence of the reason for applying, and the relevant fee. It may be submitted in Spain, through a properly authorised representative, or through the Spanish consulate responsible for your place of residence.

EU

EU citizens moving for more than three months

EU and EEA citizens who will live in Spain for more than three months normally apply in person for the Certificate of Registration as an EU Citizen, often called the "green NIE". Swiss citizens have a corresponding registration route.

The application uses form EX-18 and requires valid identification, the fee, and evidence appropriate to the applicant: employment, self-employment, or sufficient resources and healthcare cover. Students have their own evidence requirements. The application should be made within three months of entering Spain.

UK before 2021

British residents protected by the Withdrawal Agreement

British citizens who were living legally in Spain before 31 December 2020 may be protected by the Withdrawal Agreement. The Spanish Government advises eligible residents who still rely on an older green registration certificate to obtain the Article 50 TIE residence card.

The correct form and evidence depend on whether the person's residence was already documented and whether temporary or permanent status applies. We check the individual history before arranging the appointment.

UK from 2021

British citizens moving to Spain after Brexit

A British buyer can obtain an NIE for a property purchase without becoming resident. To move to Spain, however, a person arriving from 1 January 2021 normally needs the appropriate visa or residence authorisation under Spain's rules for non-EU citizens.

Once residence is approved, the TIE is the physical card that records that status. The suitable route may be based on work, self-employment, study, family, sufficient resources, or another qualifying category, so it must be selected before the TIE appointment.

Valuation Risk

If the bank values lower

If a €300,000 property is valued 10% lower, the bank may lend against €270,000 instead of the agreed price. At 60% lending, that can create an extra cash gap of about €18,000.

The risk we check before you sign

The real risk isn't the mortgage — it's the contract with the seller's agency. When you agree a price and sign with an agency, the contract terms determine what happens if the bank's official valuation later comes in below that price.

Some contracts are written in the buyer's favor and protect your deposit if this happens. Others aren't — and if the valuation falls short and your contract doesn't cover you, you can lose your deposit entirely, which can run into the thousands.

Another risk is what is already attached to the property before completion. Before signing, the buyer should check for registered charges, unpaid IBI property tax, community fees, approved community works or special assessments, pending licence or planning issues, and any seller arrangements that could affect the property after handover.

Legal support is available if you want extra reassurance. In Spain, the notary plays an important role at completion and the public records help confirm key property information, but many buyers from Europe still prefer to have an experienced qualified lawyer review the contract and advise on legal points before they sign. This is optional and can be arranged if you want that extra layer of support.

Our role: we help you check the valuation risk, review the known property obligations, bring in independent support where needed, and make sure the purchase contract has the right protection clauses before you commit your money.
