Buying in Tenerife

The tax depends on what you are buying

The Canary Islands has its own indirect-tax system. A resale and a first sale from a developer are not taxed in the same way, so we confirm the property type before giving you a completion budget.

Resale property

Plan around 6.5% ITP as the general rate

The general Canary Islands Transfer Tax rate for a property resale is 6.5%. The taxable value must be checked under the applicable valuation rules and is not always simply the price written in the contract. The buyer normally files and pays through Modelo 600.

New property

IGIC and AJD usually replace resale ITP

A first delivery by a developer is normally subject to Canary Islands IGIC rather than resale ITP. The general IGIC rate is 7%, and the notarised deed may also attract AJD, commonly 1% for operations subject to IGIC. Reduced housing rates can apply only when their detailed conditions are met.

Possible reductions

Lower rates are checked, never assumed

Canary Islands rules include reduced treatment for some qualifying habitual-home purchases, protected housing, large families, people with disabilities, single-parent families, and other defined cases. Price, income, age, family status, intended use, and deadlines can all affect eligibility.

Separate costs

Tax is not the whole completion budget

Notary, Land Registry, valuation, mortgage, legal, translation, and administration costs are separate from ITP, IGIC, and AJD. We show these separately so a tax estimate is not mistaken for the total cash required to complete.

Owning and Renting

The obligations continue after the keys

🏠

IBI is the annual local property tax

IBI is charged by the local council and is based on the property's cadastral value and the municipality's rate. The seller's latest receipt should be checked during the purchase, and the new owner should make sure future bills and direct-debit details are correct.

🌤️

Non-resident owners can have tax to file even without rent

A non-resident individual who keeps an urban property for personal use may have Spanish non-resident imputed-income tax calculated from its cadastral value. The usual tax rate is 19% for residents of the EU, Iceland, Norway, and Liechtenstein, and 24% for other non-residents, including most UK-resident owners. The calculation and Modelo 210 filing are checked for the actual ownership period and cadastral record.

🔑

Rental income is treated differently for EU and UK owners

For non-resident landlords without a Spanish permanent establishment, residents of the EU, Iceland, Norway, and Liechtenstein are generally taxed at 19% and may deduct directly connected qualifying expenses. Other non-residents, including UK residents, are generally taxed at 24% without those deductions under the current Spanish rules. Rental permission, tourist-use rules, and income tax are separate checks.

📋

Higher-value ownership and inheritance need an individual review

Spanish wealth-tax and temporary solidarity-tax exposure can depend on value, ownership, debt, residence, and treaty protection. Canary Islands inheritance and gift-tax relief can be substantial for qualifying relatives, but the relationship group, public-document requirements, connection rules, and previous gifts must be checked before relying on a reduction.

When You Sell

We prepare for the tax before completion day

Non-resident seller

The buyer normally retains 3%

When the seller is a non-resident without a Spanish permanent establishment, the buyer normally retains 3% of the agreed price and pays it to the Spanish Tax Agency using Modelo 211. This is a payment on account, not automatically the seller's final tax bill.

Capital gain

The final gain still has to be calculated

The taxable gain is calculated using the legally permitted acquisition and sale values, documented costs, and eligible improvements. A non-resident's property gain is generally taxed at 19%; the 3% retention is credited against the result, and an excess may be reclaimed.

Local council

Municipal land-value tax may also apply

The municipal tax commonly called plusvalía municipal can arise on the increase in value of urban land. Liability, method, deadlines, and any no-increase position must be checked with the relevant Tenerife municipality.

Documents

Good records protect the calculation

Purchase deeds, tax receipts, invoices for qualifying costs and improvements, rental records, and the Modelo 211 certificate can materially affect the final filing. We help organise the file before the sale completes.

Important planning note

The figures on this page are general Canary Islands planning guidance, not a personal tax calculation. Rates, reductions, taxable values, residence status, treaties, and filing deadlines must be checked against the facts and the law in force when the transaction or filing occurs.

Owning a Tenerife property does not make a person or company eligible for the ZEC 4% business regime. ZEC and company relocation are explained separately on our Move Your Company page.
